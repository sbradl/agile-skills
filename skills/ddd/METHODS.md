# Methods

Three ways for a group to surface its language. Each works on concrete cases; abstractions come after.

## Choosing a method

| | Domain Storytelling | EventStorming | Example Mapping |
|---|---|---|---|
| Looks at | One flow of work from start to finish | Everything that happens in the business, across departments | One single story or feature |
| The group | Tells a typical case sentence by sentence: who does what, with what, with whom | Each writes the events they know, then all arrange them on one timeline | Lists the rules of the story and gives concrete examples for each |
| Comes out | A recorded story of people, activities and the things they work with | A timeline of events with its most significant moments marked | Rules, examples and the questions nobody could answer |
| Surfaces mostly | The nouns and verbs of the domain; where work passes between people | Words that mean different things to different departments; context boundaries | Edge cases, exceptions and vague words inside rules |
| Fits when | The group has no shared picture of how the work flows yet | The area is large and several departments are in the room | The picture exists and one feature needs sharpening before it is built |
| Takes | A longer session per flow | The longest; a big picture | About 25 minutes per story |

Domain Storytelling and EventStorming go wide and come early; Example Mapping goes deep on one piece afterwards.

## Domain Storytelling

Experts tell one concrete case as a story while the scribe records it. Best for understanding how work flows between people.

1. Pick one typical case, the happy path first.
2. The experts tell it sentence by sentence: who does what, with what, with whom. Record each sentence, numbered, in their words.
3. When the story stalls, ask: "What happens next?", "Where do you get this information?", "How do you determine what to do next?", "How do you do that?" When someone says an unspecific "you", ask who.
4. Assumptions that keep the story on one line are spoken aloud and recorded. Variations and rules come after the story.

## EventStorming

The group collects everything that happens in the business as events on a timeline. Best for a big picture across departments.

1. Each participant writes on their own the events they know, in the past tense ("ticket sold"), so nobody biases anybody. Then the typist enters them all.
2. The group arranges the events on one timeline, discussing as they go.
3. For apparent duplicates, the group discusses whether they are the same event or the same words for different things.
4. The group marks the few most significant events; these often sit at context boundaries.

Use business words throughout: "action" rather than "command".

## Example Mapping

The group takes one story or issue and maps its rules and examples. Best for sharpening a single feature; about 25 minutes per story.

1. Pick one story.
2. Collect the rules the group knows for it.
3. Under each rule, collect concrete examples, roughly named ("the one where the customer forgot the receipt").
4. Questions nobody can answer are set aside for the group.
